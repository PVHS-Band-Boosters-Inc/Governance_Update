# reference/ — explanatory material. Nothing here is adopted.

Documents in this directory support the adopted policies in `policies/` but are **not** part of them. The Board does not adopt them, and nothing in them creates or limits an obligation.

| File | What it is | Who changes it |
|---|---|---|
| `fundraising_commentary.md` | Authorities, reasoning, and case facts behind the Fundraising and Federal Tax Compliance Policy | Any Officer, no Board action |
| `schedule_of_figures.md` | Dollar thresholds and rates the Policy operates on | Treasurer, annually and as figures change; reported to the Board, not adopted by it |

**Why the separation.** Two reasons, both practical rather than stylistic.

1. An adopted policy is a corporate instrument. Everything inside it is adopted — including any characterization of case law it contains. Those characterizations carry error risk that operative rules do not: a summary of a Tax Court opinion can be wrong in a way that "two signatures are required on all checks" cannot. Keeping them out of the adopted text means an error is corrected by editing a memo rather than by discovering that the corporation formally adopted a misstatement of law. Materials furnished to the school may become public records (District Guidelines §IV), which raises the cost of that error.

2. The figures move. The Form W-2G reporting threshold is now adjusted for inflation annually. Figures embedded in an adopted policy force a Board resolution every year or leave the Board holding a policy it knows is stale. The Schedule is expressly maintainable by the Treasurer without amending the Policy (Policy §12).
