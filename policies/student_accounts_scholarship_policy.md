# Student Accounts, Fair Share, and Scholarship Policy
### PVHS Band Boosters, Inc. — DRAFT for adoption by the Board of Directors

## 1. Purpose

Protects the corporation's 501(c)(3) status in the area where booster organizations most often lose it: private benefit through individual fundraising credits, and insider-influenced scholarship awards. The federal authority behind the rules in Sections 2 and 6, together with the excise-tax exposure that falls on individual Officers, is set out in the Fundraising and Federal Tax Compliance Policy §§2–4.

## 2. Fundraising Proceeds Benefit the Program as a Whole

All fundraising proceeds are the corporation's funds and benefit the Program as a whole. **The corporation does not credit fundraising proceeds to individual student accounts in proportion to a family's fundraising participation, and does not reduce an individual family's dues, fees, or trip costs based on the amount that family raised.** Participation in fundraising is encouraged for all families and required of none as a condition of a student's participation benefits.

This rule is fixed at member level in **Bylaws Article XVII** and cannot be changed by resolution of the Board.

**Authority.** An organization is not operated exclusively for exempt purposes "unless it serves a public rather than a private interest," Treas. Reg. §1.501(c)(3)-1(d)(1)(ii), and private benefit — unlike inurement — does not require that the beneficiary be an insider. In *Capital Gymnastics Booster Club, Inc. v. Commissioner*, T.C. Memo. 2013-193 (Aug. 26, 2013), the Tax Court sustained revocation of a booster club's exemption where mandatory assessments of "$600 to $1,400 per year per child" could be worked off through fundraising, points worth $10 each were credited against the assessment, and the club "awarded $32,920 of the net profit, or approximately 93%, to families that participated in the fundraising or who filled board positions" — about 46% of member families. The court's objection was that the club "rigorously assured that its fundraising did not generally benefit all the child-athletes in its programs but rather benefited only the children of parents who did the fundraising." The Service reached the same conclusion for booster clubs generally in PMTA 2011-04 (POSTS-117415-10, Nov. 24, 2010).

**What is permitted.** Applying proceeds to the general Program budget so that the fair share falls **for every family equally**; need-based hardship waivers and scholarships under Sections 4 and 5; and non-financial recognition of volunteers. The same opinion identifies these as distinguishing features rather than mere good practice, contrasting the club before it with "a circumstance in which the individual's contribution of his share of the cost is optional or where scholarships are made available for those who cannot afford the cost." See the Fundraising and Federal Tax Compliance Policy §2.

## 3. Dues and Assessments

Dues and Program assessments are set by the Board under Bylaws Article XIII, applied uniformly to similarly situated members, and are payments for the Program year — never a target that individual fundraising "works off" (Bylaws Art. XVII). Dues, fair share, assessments, and trip payments are deductible only to the extent they exceed the fair market value of the return benefits the student receives — which, because these charges are set to cover those very costs, means **in practice no deduction is available**. No Officer or volunteer may represent otherwise (Fundraising and Federal Tax Compliance Policy §4).

## 4. Hardship Waivers

The Board publishes hardship-waiver criteria annually. Applications go to the Treasurer, are decided by two Officers (neither with a student in the applicant's grade-level cohort where practicable), are confidential, and are reported to the Board only in aggregate. No student is excluded from Booster-supported Program participation solely for demonstrated inability to pay (Bylaws Art. XIII).

## 5. Scholarships and Financial Assistance

Awards (including banquet, trip, or senior scholarships) are made from Board-approved budget lines under written, published criteria (need- and/or merit-based), decided by a committee of persons **without a student in contention for the award**. A Director or Officer whose student is an applicant is recused from every stage. Awards, criteria, and the decision process are documented; individual applicant financials remain confidential.

Recusal here is not a formality. An award directed to the family of an Officer or Director is a transaction with a disqualified person and, if the award is not made on the same published criteria applied to everyone else, is an excess benefit transaction carrying excise tax on that individual personally and on the Board members who knowingly approved it (IRC §4958; Fundraising and Federal Tax Compliance Policy §3).

## 6. Restricted Gifts for Individuals

The corporation does not accept gifts earmarked for a named student. The reason is not administrative: a contribution over which the charity holds no discretion and control is treated as a gift to the individual rather than to the charity and is **not deductible to the donor**. *See* Rev. Rul. 62-113. Accepting such a gift therefore both misleads the donor and moves corporate funds toward private benefit.

Gifts restricted to the scholarship program generally are accepted and tracked as restricted funds under the Financial Controls Policy §8.

## 7. District Coordination

Charges, waivers, and assistance are administered consistent with St. Johns County School District policy on school-program participation charges; conflicts between this Policy and district requirements are escalated to the Board.
