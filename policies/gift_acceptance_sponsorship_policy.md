# Gift Acceptance and Sponsorship Policy
### PVHS Band Boosters, Inc. — DRAFT for adoption by the Board of Directors

## 1. Purpose

Governs acceptance and acknowledgment of donations and business sponsorships, protecting donors' deductions and the corporation's tax posture. Fundraising *design* — what the corporation may run and how proceeds may be applied — is governed by the Fundraising and Federal Tax Compliance Policy; this Policy governs what comes in the door and what the corporation says back.

## 2. What We Accept

Cash and cash equivalents; publicly documented in-kind goods and services useful to the Program; sponsorships under Section 4. The Board must approve before acceptance: real property, vehicles, securities, gifts with restrictions beyond the scholarship or trip programs, and any gift raising legal, safety, or reputational concerns.

**Gifts earmarked for a named student are not accepted.** A contribution over which the corporation holds no discretion and control is treated as a gift to the individual rather than to the charity and is not deductible to the donor (Rev. Rul. 62-113); accepting one both misleads the donor and channels corporate funds to private benefit (Student Accounts Policy §6; Fundraising and Federal Tax Compliance Policy §4).

## 3. Acknowledgments (IRC §170(f)(8))

The Treasurer issues a written acknowledgment for every gift of $250 or more, stating the amount (or description of property, without valuation) and whether goods or services were provided in return, with a good-faith estimate of their value. For fundraising events, quid pro quo notices state the deductible portion where the payment exceeds $75. Acknowledgments go out within 30 days and by January 31 for the prior calendar year.

**Not every payment to the corporation is a gift.** Dues, fair share, assessments, trip payments, and fees paid for the benefit of the payer's own student are payments for goods and services and are generally not deductible; the corporation does not acknowledge them as charitable contributions and no Officer or volunteer may represent that they are (Fundraising and Federal Tax Compliance Policy §4). Where a single payment covers both a fee and a gift, the acknowledgment separates them.

## 4. Sponsorships: Acknowledgment vs. Advertising (IRC §513(i))

Sponsor recognition **may** include: the sponsor's name, logo, location, telephone, website link, and value-neutral descriptions of its products or services. Sponsor recognition **may not** include: qualitative or comparative language ("the best...", "official pizza of..."), price information, endorsements, or calls to action ("visit ... today!"). Recognition that crosses the line is advertising and generates unrelated business taxable income; the Treasurer tracks any such arrangements separately and reports to the Board before renewal. Sponsorship amounts contingent on attendance, ratings, or sales are not accepted. Any use of the school's name, logo, mascot, or trademark in sponsorship or fundraising materials is covered by the Principal's annual written authorization (Bylaws Art. XVI; District Relations Policy §2), and materials for events on school property respect the District's exclusive beverage vendor arrangement.

Section 513(i) is one exclusion among several. Concessions, spirit-wear sales, vendor nights, and the online store are screened separately under the unrelated-business analysis in the Fundraising and Federal Tax Compliance Policy §5, and unrelated business gross income of $1,000 or more in a year triggers Form 990-T.

## 5. Valuation of In-Kind Gifts

The corporation acknowledges in-kind gifts by description only; valuation is the donor's responsibility. The Treasurer records a good-faith internal value for the books. Donated goods sold at a fundraiser may also affect the unrelated-business analysis (IRC §513(a)(3)).

## 6. Solicitation Compliance

All public solicitation (website "Donate" links, sponsorship drives, public events) proceeds only while the corporation's registration or exemption status under the Florida Solicitation of Contributions Act (ch. 496, Fla. Stat.) is current; the Treasurer verifies status annually and before each major campaign.

Solicitation tied to a drawing, raffle, or other game of chance is **not** conducted under this Policy. It requires prior Board approval and must satisfy section 849.0935, Florida Statutes — including the prohibition on requiring any payment or contribution as a condition of entry and the requirement that every ticket and advertisement state that no purchase or contribution is necessary (Fundraising and Federal Tax Compliance Policy §6).

## 7. Annual Materials Review

Before each registration window, the Treasurer reviews the registration form, website donation and store pages, sponsorship packet, and event solicitations against Sections 2 through 6, and reports exceptions to the Board.
