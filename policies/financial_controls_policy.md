# Financial Controls Policy
### PVHS Band Boosters, Inc. — DRAFT for adoption by the Board of Directors

## 1. Purpose

Protects charitable assets, the volunteers who handle them, and the Officers whose personal liability rides on how money is handled. Board approval of a budget is governance; these controls are how the budget is executed safely.

## 2. Budget

The Board adopts an annual Program budget (Bylaws Art. II) before or promptly after fiscal year start. The Treasurer reports quarterly to the Board: actual vs. budget, cash position, and any variance over 10% or $1,000 in a category. Unbudgeted expenditures over $500 require prior Board approval (Bylaws Art. II), and no person other than the President or a Board-designated Officer may execute an instrument binding the corporation (Bylaws Art. VI). The President and Treasurer acting jointly may authorize up to $1,000 in a genuine emergency where written consent of the Board is not practicable in time (Bylaws Art. II); the Treasurer documents the circumstances contemporaneously, not at the next meeting, and the expenditure is reported to the Board and minuted. Emergency authority does not relax the two-signature disbursement rule in Section 3 — it substitutes for prior Board approval, not for payment controls.

## 3. Segregation of Duties

No single person both initiates and completes any financial transaction end-to-end:
- **Checks and disbursements:** Only elected Officers may be authorized to sign checks. **Two signatures are required on all checks** (District Guidelines §V). Authorized signers may not be related to each other or live in the same household. Electronic disbursements require documented approval by two Officers, applied to the same standard. Unbudgeted disbursements over $500 additionally follow Bylaws Art. II; commitments within the Board-approved budget need no separate vote (Bylaws Art. VI). Recurring budgeted payments may be pre-approved by resolution, listed by payee and cap.
- **Debit and ATM cards.** A debit or ATM card is not issued on a corporate account except by Board resolution naming the cardholder and setting a per-transaction and monthly limit. No more than one such card is outstanding on any account at a time, held by the Treasurer. A debit or ATM card is never used for cash withdrawals except as the authorizing resolution specifically permits. Because a card transaction carries none of the two-signature control that applies to checks, all card activity is reviewed monthly as part of the non-signatory statement review and reconciliation below, not merely reconciled after the fact. The current debit/ATM cardholder is reported to the Principal annually under Bylaws Art. XVI.
- **Bank accounts:** accounts are held in the corporation's name under its own EIN — never the school's or District's — with the school's address as the primary account address, per District Guidelines. A **non-signatory** member reviews the monthly bank statements and signs them as evidence of review (District Guidelines §V).
- **Monthly reporting to the Principal:** copies of the bank statements and the treasurer's report are provided to the Principal monthly (District Guidelines §V) regardless of the Treasurer's quarterly reporting cadence to the Board — the Principal-facing report is a District requirement and is prepared monthly even in months between Board reports.
- **Reconciliation:** the Treasurer reconciles all accounts monthly; a different Officer reviews and signs the reconciliation.
- **No signature stamps; no signing blank checks; no shared login credentials.** Officers shall not use personal accounts (e.g., personal Venmo/PayPal/Zelle) to receive corporate funds.

## 4. Cash Handling

Two unrelated volunteers count all event/concession/gate cash together at the point of collection, sign a count sheet, and seal the funds; deposits are made within three (3) business days; the count sheet is matched to the deposit by someone who did not count. Startup cash boxes are logged out and back in.

## 5. Online Store and Payment Processors

Processor accounts (e.g., BoosterHub, Square, Stripe, PayPal) are owned by the corporation, not an individual; two Officers hold admin access; payout accounts are corporate bank accounts only; monthly processor settlement reports are reconciled to the bank.

## 6. Program Staff and Designer Payments; Worker Classification

Every paid staff member or designer has a **written agreement** approved by the Board (rate, scope, term) before work begins. The Treasurer, with professional advice where needed, classifies each worker (employee vs. independent contractor) under IRS common-law standards, documents the analysis, and issues W-2s or 1099s accordingly. The analysis shall expressly address who directs the work: under Bylaws Art. III the Band Director gives artistic and instructional direction to Program staff, while the corporation pays them and approves their agreements (Bylaws Art. XVI). Behavioral control resting with someone other than the payer is a fact the common-law test weighs, and it is not resolved by what the written agreement calls the worker. **Personal exposure note:** withheld payroll taxes are trust funds; responsible persons (typically the Treasurer and President) are personally liable under IRC §6672 regardless of any state-law immunity. Payments to workers are never made in cash.

**Confirm the information-return threshold each year rather than working from memory.** It changed for payments made after December 31, 2025 and is now indexed; the current figure is in `reference/schedule_of_figures.md` (A3). The threshold governs only the corporation's filing obligation. It does not change the worker-classification analysis, does not change the recipient's obligation to report the income, and does not license paying a worker below the threshold in order to avoid a return. Where classification is contested or a record would be useful, the corporation files voluntarily (Fundraising and Federal Tax Compliance Policy §8).

## 7. Reimbursements

Pre-approval for anticipated expenses; itemized receipts within 30 days; approved by an Officer other than the payee; Officer reimbursements approved by a different Officer. No reimbursement of the approving Officer by the requesting Officer in the same batch.

Any payment, reimbursement, discount, waiver, or award that benefits an Officer, a Director, or a member of either's family is a transaction with a disqualified person. If it exceeds fair value or departs from published criteria, it is an excess benefit transaction carrying excise tax on the recipient personally and on each Board member who knowingly approved it (IRC §4958; Fundraising and Federal Tax Compliance Policy §3; rates in `reference/schedule_of_figures.md`). Such transactions proceed only through the Conflict of Interest Policy, with the basis for the amount documented.

## 8. Restricted Funds

Funds raised or donated for a stated purpose (scholarships, a specific trip, equipment) are tracked separately and spent only for that purpose; reclassification requires Board action and, where practical, donor consent.

## 9. Annual Independent Review; Transition

An independent CPA review (not necessarily a full audit) is obtained annually and presented to the Board. The Treasurer files the Form 990 series return by the 15th day of the 5th month after fiscal year end, files Form 990-T where unrelated business gross income reaches the threshold in `reference/schedule_of_figures.md`, confirms that the three most recent returns and the exemption application remain posted for public inspection under IRC §6104(d), and prepares the annual report on the District's prescribed form and assembles the Article XVI annual submission package, including the final bank statement for the period including June 30. At each officer transition: signers updated at the bank within 15 days, credentials rotated, records transferred per the Document Retention Policy, and a transition reconciliation signed by outgoing and incoming Treasurers.

## 10. Sales Tax

The Treasurer determines, with professional advice, which sales (merchandise, spirit wear, concessions) require Florida sales tax collection or qualify for exemption, registers as required, and remits on time. The corporation acquires and uses its own sales tax exemption number where eligible and never uses the School Board's or the school's exemption number (District Guidelines §III) — collected tax is state money; responsible persons carry personal exposure for unremitted amounts (§§212.15, 213.29, Fla. Stat.).

## 11. Gaming and Drawings — Additional Controls

No raffle, drawing, 50/50, or other game of chance is conducted without prior written Board approval under the Fundraising and Federal Tax Compliance Policy §6. Where one is approved, these controls apply in addition to Section 4:

- **Ticket accountability.** Ticket stock is pre-numbered and logged out to and back from each seller; unsold and voided tickets are returned and reconciled to proceeds by someone who did not sell.
- **Free-entry parity.** Entries received without payment, which Florida law requires the corporation to accept on the same terms (§849.0935), are pooled and drawn identically to all others, and the count of each is recorded in the minutes.
- **Prize custody and release.** Prizes are held by an Officer who is not conducting the drawing. **A completed Form W-9 is obtained from any winner at or above the reporting threshold before the prize is released** — after release, the corporation has no leverage and bears the withholding itself.
- **Withholding funded in advance.** Where a prize could produce proceeds above the withholding trigger in `reference/schedule_of_figures.md`, the approved plan identifies the budget line that funds the withholding, because the corporation owes it whether or not it is collected from the winner. Noncash prizes are the common trap (Commentary §5).
- **No Officer, Director, or member of either's household** may win a prize in a drawing the corporation conducts, and none may participate in selecting the winner.
- **Records.** Ticket logs, the drawing record, W-2Gs, and W-9s are retained under the Document Retention Policy on the accounting-records schedule.
