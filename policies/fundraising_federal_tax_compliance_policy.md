# Fundraising and Federal Tax Compliance Policy
### PVHS Band Boosters, Inc. — DRAFT for adoption by the Board of Directors

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This Policy governs how the corporation raises money and how it applies the proceeds, under federal tax law. Florida corporate-law obligations are in the Bylaws; District obligations are in the District Relations and Facilities Policy.

Two companion documents are **not part of this Policy and are not adopted with it**:

- **`reference/fundraising_commentary.md`** — the authorities behind these rules, the reasoning, and the facts of the leading case. Explanatory only.
- **`reference/schedule_of_figures.md`** — the dollar thresholds and rates this Policy operates on. The Treasurer maintains and updates the Schedule without Board action or amendment of this Policy (§12).

## 2. All Proceeds Are Corporate Funds; No Individual Fundraising Credits

All fundraising proceeds are the corporation's funds and are applied to the Program as a whole.

The corporation shall not: track, credit, or settle fundraising proceeds by family; reduce any individual family's dues, fair share, assessments, or trip costs by reference to the amount that family raised, solicited, or worked; or maintain individual student accounts funded by fundraising proceeds. No student's participation in or benefit from the Program shall be conditioned on the family's fundraising participation.

This rule is fixed in **Bylaws Article XVII** and may not be varied by resolution of the Board.

The following are permitted:

- Applying proceeds to the general Program budget so that the fair share falls for every similarly situated family equally;
- Setting a lower fair share in a successful fundraising year, announced and applied uniformly;
- Need-based hardship waivers and scholarships under the Student Accounts, Fair Share, and Scholarship Policy;
- Non-financial recognition of volunteers carrying no economic value;
- Sponsorship and donation solicitation under the Gift Acceptance and Sponsorship Policy.

*Authority:* Treas. Reg. §1.501(c)(3)-1(d)(1)(ii); *Capital Gymnastics Booster Club, Inc. v. Commissioner*, T.C. Memo. 2013-193 (Aug. 26, 2013); PMTA 2011-04 (POSTS-117415-10). See Commentary §1.

## 3. Transactions with Officers, Directors, and Their Families (IRC §4958)

Officers and Directors are disqualified persons as to the corporation (Treas. Reg. §53.4958-3(c)).

No payment, reimbursement, discount, waiver, award, or other transfer of value to an Officer, a Director, or a member of either's family shall be approved except through the Conflict of Interest Policy, with the basis for the amount documented by comparability data. A transfer exceeding fair value, or departing from published criteria, is an excess benefit transaction carrying excise tax on the recipient personally and on each Board member who knowingly approved it, at the rates in the Schedule of Figures.

These liabilities are personal. They are not defeated by indemnification under Bylaws Article XII or by the director-liability provisions of chapter 617, Florida Statutes. See Commentary §2.

## 4. Representations About Deductibility

No Officer, Director, or volunteer shall represent that any payment to the corporation is tax-deductible. The corporation does not advise members on their returns.

The corporation's published materials, receipts, and acknowledgments shall be accurate as follows:

- **Dues, fair share, assessments, trip payments, and fees** for the payer's own student are deductible only to the extent the payment exceeds the fair market value of the return benefits received and the payer intended the excess as a gift. The corporation shall not describe them as charitable contributions.
- **Fundraising event payments** are deductible only to the extent the payment exceeds the fair market value of what the payer receives. The corporation states the deductible portion for quid pro quo contributions above the §6115 threshold, and issues contemporaneous written acknowledgments at or above the §170(f)(8) threshold (Gift Acceptance and Sponsorship Policy §3). Both thresholds are in the Schedule of Figures.
- **Gifts earmarked for a named student** are not accepted and are not deductible to the donor (Rev. Rul. 62-113; Student Accounts Policy §6; Gift Acceptance Policy §2).
- **Volunteer time and services** are not deductible.

The Treasurer reviews the registration form, website donation and store pages, sponsorship packet, and event solicitations against this Section before each registration window and reports exceptions to the Board. See Commentary §3.

## 5. Unrelated Business Activity (IRC §§512–513)

The Treasurer shall screen each recurring fundraiser for unrelated business income — trade or business, regularly carried on, not substantially related to the exempt purpose — and document the conclusion in the annual budget cycle. The applicable exclusions are the volunteer labor exception (§513(a)(1)), the donated merchandise exception (§513(a)(3)), the "not regularly carried on" test, and qualified sponsorship payments (§513(i); Gift Acceptance and Sponsorship Policy §4).

Where the volunteer labor exception is relied on, the Treasurer shall record who performed the work. Paid staff running an activity defeats the exception.

The corporation files Form 990-T where gross unrelated business income reaches the threshold in the Schedule of Figures, and makes estimated payments where required. Any conclusion that a material fundraiser is unrelated is reported to the Board before the activity is repeated. See Commentary §4.

## 6. Games of Chance — Board Pre-Clearance Required

No raffle, drawing, 50/50, door-prize drawing, bingo, casino night, or similar game of chance shall be conducted in the corporation's name, with its funds, or at its events without **prior written Board approval recorded in the minutes**, granted on a written plan addressing each requirement of this Section. The Band Director and the Principal are notified before approval; events on school property additionally follow the District Relations and Facilities Policy.

**Conditions of approval.** The plan shall establish that:

1. The corporation's registration or exemption under chapter 496, Florida Statutes, is current — a condition of the authorization itself under §849.0935(2) — and its IRS determination letter is current (§849.0935(1)(b)).
2. **No payment, entry fee, donation, proof of purchase, or contribution is required as a condition of entry or of winning** (§849.0935(4)(b)). A *suggested* minimum donation is permitted and shall be labeled as suggested. Free entry is accepted from any person on terms identical to those for contributors.
3. Every brochure, advertisement, notice, ticket, and entry blank conspicuously discloses the rules, the corporation's full legal name and principal address, the source of the prize fund, the date, time, and place of the drawing, and **that no purchase or contribution is necessary** (§849.0935(3)(e)).
4. The drawing is not conditioned on a minimum number of entries or a minimum level of contributions, and contributors and non-contributors are treated identically.
5. Federal reporting and withholding are provided for: Form W-2G where winnings meet the reporting threshold and the 300× multiple; regular gambling withholding on proceeds above the withholding threshold; backup withholding for a winner who does not furnish a correct taxpayer identification number. All thresholds and rates are in the Schedule of Figures.
6. **The plan identifies the budget line funding any withholding the corporation may owe.** Withholding is the corporation's obligation whether or not collected from the winner.
7. A completed Form W-9 is obtained from any winner at or above the reporting threshold **before the prize is released**.
8. Gaming revenue is reported on the Form 990 series, including Schedule G where applicable, and the §5 unrelated-business analysis has been applied.
9. Handling controls under the Financial Controls Policy §11 are in place.

**Standing item.** Before the first drawing approved under this Policy, the Treasurer shall review the corporation's past drawings against conditions 1–4 and report the results to the Board.

The Treasurer confirms current thresholds against the current-year Instructions for Forms W-2G and 5754 before each approved drawing. See Commentary §5.

## 7. Federal Public Disclosure (IRC §6104(d))

The corporation shall make available to **any person** who asks, without regard to membership or stated purpose:

- each annual information return, during the three-year period beginning on that return's filing due date, excluding the names and addresses of contributors; and
- the application for exemption and supporting documents, together with the IRS determination letter, with no time limit.

In-person requests are satisfied the day of the request, subject to the regulatory unusual-circumstances exception; written requests within 30 days.

The Treasurer shall maintain these documents on the corporation's website in the required format. **Posting satisfies the obligation to provide copies. It does not satisfy the separate obligation to permit inspection**, and the Treasurer shall identify where and when inspection may occur. Failure carries per-day penalties assessed against the individual responsible, plus a separate penalty for willful failure. See Commentary §6.

## 8. Information Returns for Program Staff and Designers

Payments to individuals for services follow the Financial Controls Policy §6. The Treasurer shall confirm the current information-return threshold each year against the Schedule of Figures rather than relying on prior practice. The threshold governs only the corporation's filing obligation; it does not affect worker classification or the recipient's obligation to report the income. The corporation may file voluntarily where classification is contested or a record would be useful. See Commentary §7.

## 9. Annual Compliance Calendar

The Treasurer maintains this calendar and reports against it to the Board at least annually.

| Item | Timing |
|---|---|
| Form 990 series return (and Form 990-T if required) | By the 15th day of the 5th month after fiscal year end |
| Review of registration form, website, and sponsorship materials (§4) | Before the registration window opens |
| Unrelated-business screen of each recurring fundraiser (§5) | With the annual budget |
| Confirm returns and exemption application posted; inspection location identified (§7) | With the annual financial statements (Bylaws Art. XIV) |
| Florida Solicitation of Contributions Act status (ch. 496, Fla. Stat.) | Annually and before each major campaign |
| **Update the Schedule of Figures** and report changes to the Board | Annually, and before each approved drawing |

## 10. Escalation

Any Officer, Director, committee chair, or member aware of a fundraising practice inconsistent with this Policy shall report it under the Whistleblower Policy. A practice that has already occurred is not cured by silence; the Board shall document it, correct it prospectively, and obtain professional advice on whether correction or disclosure is required.

## 11. Professional Advice

This Policy is not tax advice to any member. The Board shall obtain advice from a qualified tax professional before adopting any fundraising design not plainly addressed here, and before any first-time gaming activity.

## 12. Interpretation; Maintenance of the Schedule

The Commentary is explanatory and is not part of this Policy; nothing in it creates or limits an obligation, and the Board does not adopt it.

The Schedule of Figures supplies the dollar thresholds and rates referenced above. **The Treasurer shall update the Schedule as figures change and report the changes to the Board at the next meeting. Updating the Schedule is not an amendment of this Policy and requires no Board action.** Where the Schedule conflicts with the governing statute, regulation, or current-year form instructions, those control and the Treasurer corrects the Schedule.

Amendment of this Policy requires Board action, except that no Board action may vary Bylaws Article XVII.
