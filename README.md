# PVHS Band Boosters — Governance Revision Package

**Status: DRAFTS.** Nothing in this repository is in effect. These are proposed documents for board and counsel review, prepared August 2026 in response to the Florida Nonprofit Corporation Act (Ch. 2026-168, Laws of Fla., effective July 1, 2026) and the governance review in the board's project files. **This package is not legal advice; have Florida nonprofit counsel review before adoption.**

## What's here

- `bylaws.md` — proposed Amended and Restated Bylaws. **Read the git history:** the first commit is the current bylaws verbatim; every commit after it is one amendment with its statutory driver and reasoning in the commit message (`git log -p bylaws.md`).
- `articles_of_incorporation_RESTATED_DRAFT.md` — restated Articles curing the federal organizational-test defect (no dissolution clause in the 2012 Articles; Florida is not a Rev. Proc. 82-2 state). **This is the highest-stakes document in the package.**
- `policies/` — eleven board policies, one commit each, rationale in each commit message. The District Relations and Facilities policy operationalizes the St. Johns County School District's *Guidelines for School Support Organizations*, which the bylaws (Art. XVI) now incorporate. The Fundraising and Federal Tax Compliance policy carries the federal layer — private benefit, deductibility, unrelated business income, gaming, and public disclosure — which the rest of the package touched only glancingly. The Committees and Individual Positions policy carries the operational detail (the specific committee roster, duties, and Officer-chair pairings) that used to sit in Bylaws Article VII, now a short enabling clause — see the design-choices table below.
- `reference/` — **not adopted.** Explanatory material supporting the policies: `fundraising_commentary.md` (authorities, case facts, reasoning) and `schedule_of_figures.md` (every dollar threshold and rate, maintained by the Treasurer). See `reference/README.md` for why these are separate.

### What gets adopted, and what does not

| | Adopted by | Changed by |
|---|---|---|
| `articles_of_incorporation_RESTATED_DRAFT.md` | Members (or board — see step 4) + state filing | Same |
| `bylaws.md` | Members | Members (Art. IX) |
| `policies/*.md` | Board resolution | Board resolution |
| `reference/*.md` | **Nobody — explanatory only** | Treasurer or any Officer, no Board action |

The last row is the point of the split. An adopted policy is a corporate instrument, and everything in it — including any characterization of case law — is adopted with it. Those characterizations carry error risk that operative rules do not, and the figures they rest on are now indexed annually. Keeping both out of the adopted text means a correction is an edit, not a resolution.

### Three layers of law, three enforcement paths

The package addresses obligations that come from different places and are enforced differently, and the board should not treat them as interchangeable. **Chapter 617, Florida Statutes** governs how the corporation is run; failure shows up as invalid action, personal director liability, and administrative dissolution. **The District's SSO Guidelines** govern the corporation's permission to operate as a school support organization; failure shows up as revoked authorization and a forced name change (Bylaws Art. XVI). **Federal tax law** governs the exemption itself and the personal excise-tax exposure of individual officers; failure shows up as retroactive revocation and IRC §4958 taxes that no state-law immunity and no indemnification reach. The third layer is the one boosters most often lose, and it is lost over fundraising design rather than over paperwork.

## Adoption sequence (recommended)

1. **Now, board only (unanimous written consent of all Directors then in office):** adopt the eleven policies in `policies/`. **Do not adopt anything in `reference/`** — receive it and note it in the minutes instead. Two caveats. The Conflict of Interest Policy's approval safe harbor (its §6) is dormant until step 3 — the current Bylaws Article XI prohibition controls until members replace it. And the Fundraising and Federal Tax Compliance Policy is adoptable and effective now, but its central rule is only as durable as the board that adopted it until Bylaws Article XVII is ratified at step 3.
2. **Now, no vote needed:** confirm D&O insurance; verify Sunbiz filing history (any post-2012 articles amendments?), registered agent, and FDACS Ch. 496 registration status. Also: have the Treasurer review whether past raffles or drawings satisfied §849.0935, Fla. Stat., and post the last three Form 990 returns and the exemption application for public inspection under IRC §6104(d) if they are not already up.
3. **Member action:** adopt the restated bylaws — either at a General Meeting under current Article IX (Executive Committee review → 14 days' notice → two-thirds of members present; note the current clause has no quorum, which is itself a reason to prefer the next option) or by **written consent under §617.0701(4)** (two-thirds of all members), which moots any meeting-validity challenge. **The binding constraint on that route is the 90-day window:** §617.0701(4)(a) requires every consent to be delivered within 90 days of the earliest-dated one, so the signature drive has a hard clock running from the first signature — do not start collecting before the campaign is staffed. Target: adopted before the Spring Semester annual meeting so incoming Officers take office under the new structure.

   **The adopting resolution must carry membership transition language.** Article IV as restated conditions membership on completing both the corporation's own registration and District volunteer registration. That gate applies from the instant of adoption, so every parent who has not completed both ceases to be a member at that moment — collapsing the denominator for the 10% quorum, the 10% special-meeting demand, and the two-thirds amendment vote in the same breath as the vote that created them. Adopt with language substantially as follows:

   > RESOLVED, that each person who is a member of the corporation immediately before the effective date of these Amended and Restated Bylaws continues as a member through the end of the Program year in which they are adopted, without regard to the registration requirements of Article IV; and that the membership qualifications of Article IV first determine membership for the Program year beginning after that date.

   This turns a cliff into a registration drive with a deadline. Record it in the minutes alongside the vote. It lives here rather than in the Bylaws because it is a one-time transition, and permanent text describing a single past event reads as a defect to every future reader.
4. **Articles:** adopt the restated Articles per §617.1002 (counsel question: §617.1002(2) may permit board-only adoption since members are not expressly entitled to vote on articles amendments) and file with the Department of State.

## Design choices the board should consciously ratify or change

| Choice | Where | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Board = the 5 elected Officers **plus up to 2 member-elected At-Large Directors** (seats always on the ballot; unfilled if nobody runs; never filled by the Board); Band Director non-voting advisor | Bylaws Art. VIII | Revert to Officers-only; or seat the Band Director as a voting director (not recommended — dual-duty and conflict problems) |
| Board may remove an At-Large Director for cause, but the removal goes to the members for ratification automatically | Art. VIII | Members-only removal of At-Large Directors (stronger oversight independence, slower); or Board removal with only the demand-driven backstop |
| Member quorum 10% | Art. IV | Any figure; statute supplies none |
| Emergency spending: President + Treasurer jointly, up to $1,000, only where Board written consent is not practicable in time | Art. II | Omit it and rely on majority written consent under Art. VIII (now signable electronically); or set a lower cap |
| Limited **directed** proxies permitted (proxy holder must be a member, one proxy per member, meeting-specific, no discretionary proxies); absentee electronic ballots permitted; both count toward quorum | Art. IV | Prohibit proxies entirely (the earlier choice in this package); or permit general discretionary proxies on the statutory default terms (11-month validity) |
| Removal of an Officer: members by 2/3 of votes cast, **or** the Board for cause by 2/3 of Directors, with a 30-day member-demand backstop; Board may suspend an Officer's authority pending determination | Art. V | Members-only removal (slower; more independent of the Board). Note the §617.0808 gap-filler for a member-elected director is a majority of **all** member votes, not of votes cast — a harder bar than what is drafted |
| Removal of an At-Large Director: same two tracks, but a Board removal goes to the members for ratification **automatically**, no demand required | Art. VIII | Members-only removal of At-Large Directors |
| No individual fundraising credits; no fundraising-participation requirement | **Art. XVII (new)** | None that is safe. The board could leave this at policy level only, where a future board can repeal it by resolution — the reason it is proposed at member level is precisely that the people who benefit from repealing it are the people who would vote |
| Dissolution: generic 501(c)(3)/governmental distribution, **no named preference for the District or this band program** | Art. X | Name the St. Johns County School District (for the PVHS band program) as the preferred first recipient, with 501(c)(3) and governmental fallbacks — the prior text, and equally compliant |
| Membership gated: parent/guardian of an enrolled student **and** corporation registration **and** current District volunteer registration, as an annual term that expires rather than a status that must be terminated | Art. IV | Revert to automatic membership for all parents (larger, less certain denominator); or convert to a **nonmember** corporation — a live option discussed in the governance review; simpler governance, but requires member approval to adopt and is a bigger change than this package attempts |
| Committee roster moved from Bylaws Article VII to a board-amendable policy; Article VII keeps only the enabling clause, the reporting rule, and the cross-reference extending Article V (no compensation) and Article XI (conflicts) to committee chairs, committee members, and individual-position holders | Art. VII; `policies/committees_and_positions_policy.md` | Keep the itemized committee list in the Bylaws, at the cost of a two-thirds member vote every time a committee is added, renamed, or retired |
| Committee chairs and individual-position holders sign the annual COI disclosure statement; ordinary committee members are bound by the same disclose-and-recuse duty but do not sign the annual paperwork | Art. XI | Require every committee member to sign annually — more complete, but a real administrative load across ~19 committees of parent volunteers |

## Not included (backlog — mostly district-dependent)

Three operational policies remain deliberately undrafted: **child safety / chaperone conduct, travel & transportation, and student media/privacy.** The reason is a sourcing gap, not an oversight: the district's *SSO Guidelines* (fully incorporated above) regulate the organization — authorization, finances, insurance, facilities, and volunteer *registration* — but contain no conduct-level rules, and the district's public volunteer page confirms registration mechanics (background/sex-offender check, 3-year renewal) while publishing **no** chaperone ratios, overnight-travel rules, or student-driver/insurance requirements. Drafting those policies with invented numbers would be worse than the gap; get the current chaperone/field-trip requirements from the school (Volunteer Services: 904-547-3945), and these three drafts follow in an afternoon. Also confirm which District annual-report form and Volunteer Liability Awareness questionnaire are current — the Guidelines PDF reviewed is the district's 2020 posting. An annual board calendar (filings, budget, review, disclosure statements) is worth adopting once the bylaws land.

## Figures that move

Every dollar threshold and rate the package depends on now lives in one file: **`reference/schedule_of_figures.md`**, sorted by how each figure behaves — indexed annually (re-check every year), statutory and stable, donor-side informational, and the corporation's own internal limits. It carries an as-of date, a change log, a next-review trigger, and an express instruction that the underlying source controls over the Schedule. The Treasurer maintains it **without Board action**; Fundraising Policy §12 says so explicitly, and Policy §9 puts the annual update on the compliance calendar.

Two cautions the board should inherit:

1. **IRS publications on irs.gov are not necessarily current.** Publication 3079 is the natural place to look up raffle rules, is revised 10-2018, and still shows the old $600 Form W-2G threshold. The first draft of this package repeated it; the error was caught only on adversarial re-check against the 01/2026 form instructions (commit `Corrections after adversarial verification`). Use current-year *form instructions*, not topic publications.
2. **Penalty amounts appear nowhere in the package**, deliberately. They are inflation-adjusted annually, and a document reciting them reads as authoritative while being wrong within a year.
